Three ways UCHF keeps a student in class.

Every intervention below has a name and a school attached to it — see the real examples on our Stories page.

Fee payment

Secondary & post-secondary fees

The foundation's core work: paying school fees directly so a bright student isn't sent home. Beneficiaries have come from schools including St. Teresa's Girls, Mwingi Boys, Kisasi Boys, Ngomeni Boys, Nuu Boys, St. Angela's Girls, Ndatani Girls, Kyuso Boys, Yambyu Girls and Kyondoni Girls High Schools, plus colleges such as Jordan College and Mwingi West Teachers Training College.

Inclusive support

Students living with disability

UCHF has specifically supported learners at Maii School for the Blind and Ngaaie Special School — students like Elizabeth Kakii, whose determination to keep learning inspired the foundation and community alike.

Community sponsorship

Linking individual sponsors to students

Members and well-wishers such as Mr. Enoch Nguthu have, through the foundation, fully sponsored individual students — proof that one person's goodwill, channelled through UCHF, can change a young life.

How a sponsorship happens

From identifying need to a receipt in hand

  • 01Identify. Volunteers and school contacts flag students at risk of dropping out over unpaid fees, including students with special needs.
  • 02Verify. Foundation members, including the Chairman and volunteer clergy, confirm the need directly with the school and family.
  • 03Pay. Fees are paid — sometimes by the foundation's pooled funds, sometimes by an individual sponsor working through UCHF.
  • 04Follow up. The foundation stays with the student, as with Jackson Kiema, who was supported through to a Bachelor's degree and a teaching post.

Goals we work toward

Not just fee payment — a wider network

  • Enlighten and mobilize the community to invest resourcefully in education, offering incentives where possible.
  • Create linkages between the community and other actors in education and development.
  • Build a network of like-minded people to exchange ideas and resources.
  • Act as a social-audit provider for community development projects.
